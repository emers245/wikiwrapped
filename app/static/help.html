Getting started

Wikipedia Wrapped shows what you have been reading on Wikipedia: a map of the topics, the paths you took from one article to the next, and when you read them. Everything is built from your own browsing history, on your own computer.

  1. The first time you open the app, a short setup asks which Chrome profiles to read, whether you want to add Google Takeout archives (optional), and whether to sync each time the app starts. Click Save and start at the end.
  2. The first sync reads your history, then looks up each article on Wikipedia to find its topic and its place on the map. With a lot of history this takes several minutes. Progress shows in a strip at the top of the window, and you can use the app meanwhile. When it finishes, the dashboard fills in.
  3. After that, the app syncs each time it starts (unless you turn that off), so new reading appears on its own. You can change anything later from Settings in the top-right corner.
Open the app at least once a month. Chrome deletes history older than about 90 days. The app keeps a permanent copy of every Wikipedia visit it has synced, but it can only save what Chrome still has when it syncs. If you go three months without opening it, the visits from that gap are gone for good (unless Google Takeout has them; see Setting up Google Takeout).

Reading the dashboard

The large area shows one of two views; switch with the UMAP and Threads buttons at the top left. The panels on the left summarize the same history. Hover over any article, in either view, to see its details and highlight it in the panels.

The map (UMAP)

  • Each colored dot is an article you read. Articles about similar things sit close together, so neighborhoods of the map are subjects: one corner might be history, another physics.
  • Size is how many times you visited it. Color is its topic, matching the category panel.
  • The faint grey dots are a fixed sample of Wikipedia articles that defines the map's layout. They are the same for everyone, so your map is comparable over time.
  • Scroll to zoom, drag to move around.
  • Hovering an article shows a card with its topic, your visits, when you first read it, how many times other people viewed it over the same period (n/a when that is not available), its links in the threads view, and a link to the article.

"UMAP" is the name of the method that turns article descriptions into positions on a flat map. An article only gets a position once its summary has been looked up, so for a short while after a big sync some articles are missing from the map; the header says so while that is the case.

The threads

  • Lines connect articles you read one after another: two visits less than 15 minutes apart count as one reading session, and the articles are linked in the order you read them.
  • Size shows how central an article is to your reading (its PageRank): large articles are ones many of your reading paths lead to.
  • Hover an article to highlight its connections and fade everything else, so you can follow a thread.

The panels

The Panels button hides or shows the sidebar. Drag the sidebar's right edge to make the panels bigger; double-click the edge to reset. Press Escape to clear every selection at once.

Showing only some accounts. If your history comes from more than one Google account (say, work and personal), choose which ones the dashboard shows in Settings > Show in dashboard. Hidden accounts are not deleted and keep syncing; a note in the header says how many are hidden.

Where your data come from, and what leaves your computer

What the app reads

  • Google Chrome on this computer. The app reads the history of the Chrome profiles you chose in setup. It reads a copy, so Chrome can stay open.
  • Google Takeout archives, if you add a folder for them (see below). These cover your whole Google account, including other computers and your phone, and reach further back than 90 days.
  • Google Drive, only if you set up the Advanced option.

From all of these, the app keeps only visits to Wikipedia, and only the article address, title, time, and which account it came from. Everything else in your history is ignored and never stored.

What is sent over the internet

  • The titles of the Wikipedia articles you visited are sent to Wikipedia's public services (run by the Wikimedia Foundation) to look up each article's topic, summary, and popularity. Requests are identified only as coming from "Wikipedia Wrapped". Your other browsing, your name, and your account are not sent.
  • Only if you use the Advanced Google Drive option: the app signs in to your own Google Drive (read-only) and downloads your Takeout archives from it.

Nothing else leaves your computer. The language model that places articles on the map comes with the app and runs locally. There are no accounts, no analytics, and no servers of the app's own.

Setting up Google Takeout

Optional, but worth it if you want history from before the last 90 days, or from your phone and other computers. Google Takeout exports the Chrome history saved in your Google account (when Chrome sync is on). You set it up once to repeat every two months, and point Wikipedia Wrapped at the folder where the archives arrive. No sign-in to Google is needed in the app.

Part 1: schedule the export in Google Takeout

  1. Go to takeout.google.com and sign in with the Google account whose Chrome history you want.
  2. Products you use are already ticked. Click Deselect all, then tick Chrome only.
  3. Under Chrome, click All Chrome data included and leave only History ticked. This keeps the archives small. Click OK.
  4. Scroll down and click Next step.
  5. Delivery method: choose Add to Drive. (Drive keeps the files until you delete them; a download link sent by email expires after about 7 days.)
  6. Export type (or frequency): choose Scheduled exports. Google then makes a new archive every 2 months for a year, starting now.
  7. File type: choose Zip files. The app cannot read the other type (.tgz).
  8. Leave Archive size as it is, and click Create export.

The first archive can take anywhere from a few minutes to a few days; Google emails you when it is ready. A scheduled export runs for one year; after that, set it up again.

Part 2, option A: let Google Drive for desktop bring the archives in (recommended)

  1. Install Google Drive for desktop and sign in with the same Google account.
  2. Find the folder Takeout delivers into: Takeout, in My Drive.
    • Mac: in Finder, under Locations in the sidebar, click Google Drive, then My Drive. (If Finder asks, click Enable at the top right.) The folder lives under ~/Library/CloudStorage.
    • Windows: open the Google Drive drive in File Explorer (usually G:), then My Drive.
  3. Make the folder available offline. By default Drive for desktop only downloads a file when something opens it (files that are not downloaded show a cloud icon), and the app cannot read an archive that is not on your computer while you are offline. Right-click the Takeout folder, choose Offline access, then Available offline (Windows) or Make available offline (Mac). Alternatively, keep everything on your computer: Drive for desktop Settings > Preferences > Google Drive, and under My Drive syncing options choose Mirror files.
  4. In Wikipedia Wrapped, open Settings, and under Google Takeout click Add a folder... and choose that Takeout folder. Give it a label, such as your Google email, so you can tell accounts apart. The app shows how many archives it found.

Part 2, option B: download the archives yourself

  1. When Google emails you that an archive is ready, download it. (If you chose Add to Drive, download it from Drive; a link sent by email expires after about 7 days.)
  2. Put the downloaded takeout-....zip files in one folder, for example a "Wikipedia Takeout" folder in Documents. Leave them as zips; do not unzip them.
  3. In Wikipedia Wrapped, add that folder in Settings > Google Takeout. Repeat the download every two months, when Google emails you.
What the app does with the archives. It checks the folder (and folders inside it, up to 4 levels down) at every sync, reads only the Chrome history inside each new archive, and keeps only the Wikipedia visits. It never changes, moves, or deletes your archives; once an archive has been synced you may delete it yourself, because its visits are saved. Large exports are split into several zips; only one of them holds the history, and the others are skipped quietly.

Advanced: signing in to Google Drive

Most people do not need this: a watched folder does the same job without signing in. This option has the app fetch the Takeout archives straight from Google Drive, using a Google Cloud project you create yourself. Access is read-only: the app can list and download files, and never changes or deletes anything in your Drive. The app never sees your Google password.

1. Create your own OAuth client (once, in Google Cloud Console)

  1. Go to console.cloud.google.com and create a project.
  2. In APIs and Services > Library, find Google Drive API and click Enable.
  3. Open Google Auth Platform. Set Audience to External, and fill in an app name and your email.
  4. Under Audience > Test users, add every Google account whose history you want to read.
  5. Under Data access, add the scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.readonly and nothing else.
  6. Under Clients, click Create client, choose Desktop app, and download the JSON file.

2. Connect it in Wikipedia Wrapped

  1. Open Settings, then the Advanced section.
  2. Click Choose file... and pick the JSON file you downloaded. The app checks it and keeps its own private copy.
  3. Click Add a Google account.... Your browser opens Google's sign-in; finish there and come back. Repeat for each account.
  4. Optionally enter the name of the Drive folder to search (Takeout); leave it blank to search all of Drive.
  5. Click Save. The next sync checks Drive.
About the 7-day sign-out. While your Cloud project's app is in "Testing", Google signs it out after about 7 days. The app then marks the account as needing to sign in again (in the progress strip and in Settings); click Sign in again next to it. Your local Chrome history and any watched folders keep syncing in the meantime.

Troubleshooting

No Chrome profiles found

  • The app reads Google Chrome only. Safari, Firefox, Edge and other browsers are not supported.
  • Chrome keeps its history in your user account's folder. If you have never opened Chrome while signed in to this computer as yourself, there is nothing to read yet: open Chrome once, then reopen Settings.
  • Where the app looks:
    • Mac: ~/Library/Application Support/Google/Chrome
    • Windows: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Google\Chrome\User Data
  • If you use Chrome with sync turned on, Google Takeout can still bring in your history from your Google account.

No Takeout archives found

  • The first export can take hours or days. Check your email from Google.
  • The app looks for files named takeout-...zip. A .tgz archive cannot be read: choose Zip files in Takeout.
  • Make sure you added the folder the archives are actually in. The app looks up to 4 folders deep, but skips hidden folders and the Trash.
  • With Google Drive for desktop, the archive may still be syncing to your computer. Wait for it to finish, and make the folder available offline.

Sync problems

Problems from the last sync are listed at the top of Settings, and the progress strip says how many there were. None of them stops the app from working with the history it already has. Common ones:

MessageWhat to do
"not a readable zip" The archive is still downloading or syncing, or is damaged. It is tried again at the next sync. If it keeps happening, download it again.
"could not be read ... online-only Google Drive file" Make the folder available offline in Drive for desktop, or connect to the internet and sync again.
"Folder not found" The folder is on a drive that is not connected, or it was moved or renamed. Reconnect the drive, or change the folder in Settings.
"Cannot read the folder" (Mac) macOS may be blocking access to Desktop, Documents, Downloads, or an external drive. Allow it in System Settings > Privacy & Security > Files and Folders.
"needs you to sign in again" See the 7-day sign-out; click Sign in again in Settings.
Topics or map positions missing Usually means Wikipedia could not be reached (for example, you were offline). Your visits are saved; the missing details are added at the next sync.

To try again straight away, use Settings > Syncing > Sync now.

The app does not open

The first launch after installing can take a little longer than later ones. If the window still does not appear:

  1. Look near the end of the log for an error.
  2. Run the app's self-check. It tests the installation without opening a window and prints PASS or FAIL for each part; keep its output if you ask for help. In the commands, the v0.1.0 folder is named after your version (there is only one).
    • Mac, in Terminal:
      ~/Library/WikipediaWrapped-App/v0.1.0/bin/python ~/Library/WikipediaWrapped-App/v0.1.0/share/wikipedia-wrapped/launch.py --self-test
    • Windows, in Command Prompt:
      "%LOCALAPPDATA%\WikipediaWrapped-App\v0.1.0\python.exe" "%LOCALAPPDATA%\WikipediaWrapped-App\v0.1.0\share\wikipedia-wrapped\launch.py" --self-test
  3. Uninstalling and installing again repairs a damaged installation. Your data are kept (see Uninstalling).

Viewing the logs

The app writes a log of everything it does, including every sync, to logs/app.log in its data folder (location below; the exact path is also shown at the bottom of Settings). Older logs are kept as app.log.1 to app.log.5. They are plain text: open them in any text editor, or attach them when asking for help. They contain article titles you read, so look through them before sharing.

Resetting

Quit the app first.

  • Run setup again: delete config.local.toml. The next launch shows the setup screens; your history is kept.
  • Rebuild the dashboard's database: delete data/app.db (and app.db-wal and app.db-shm next to it, if present). The next sync rebuilds it from visits.jsonl and looks the articles up again, which takes a while.
  • Start over completely: delete the whole folder.
Before deleting the folder, copy data/visits.jsonl somewhere safe. It holds every visit the app has ever saved; anything older than Chrome's 90 days exists nowhere else, unless a Takeout archive has it.

Installing, updating and uninstalling

Installing

The installers are on the project's Releases page. Download the one for your computer:

Your computerDownload the file ending in
Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later), macOS 11 or later -macOS-AppleSilicon.pkg
Mac with an Intel processor, macOS 11 or later -macOS-Intel.pkg
Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit -Windows-x64.exe

Not sure which Mac you have? Choose Apple menu > About This Mac: a Chip line means Apple silicon; a Processor line naming Intel means Intel. Skip the Source code downloads on the same page: they are the program's code, not an installer.

The installers are not yet signed by a registered developer, so your computer asks you to confirm once before running each one.

On a Mac

  1. Double-click the downloaded .pkg. The first time, macOS refuses to open it because it cannot check the developer. Close the message.
  2. Choose Apple menu > System Settings, click Privacy & Security in the sidebar, and scroll down to Security. Next to the note about the installer, click Open Anyway, enter your login password, and confirm with Open. The button is there for about an hour after you tried to open the file. On macOS 14 or earlier you can instead Control-click the .pkg in Finder, choose Open, then Open again. (On macOS 11 and 12 the setting above is in System Preferences > Security & Privacy > General.)
  3. Follow the installer. It installs for you only, without asking for an administrator password. Near the end it sits still for a few minutes while it prepares the app; notifications show it is still working.
  4. Open the app: press Command-Space, type Wikipedia Wrapped, and press Return. It is also in the Applications folder inside your home folder (in Finder, Go > Home); drag it to the Dock to keep it there.
Screenshot placeholder: Privacy & Security, Security section, with Open Anyway

On Windows

  1. Your browser may say the file is not commonly downloaded. In Microsoft Edge, open the download's ... menu, choose Keep, then Show more > Keep anyway.
  2. Double-click the .exe. Windows shows Windows protected your PC: click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer and keep its suggestions: Just Me (no administrator needed) and the folder it proposes. Installing takes a few minutes.
  4. Open the app from the Start menu or from the shortcut on your desktop.
Screenshot placeholder: Windows protected your PC, after clicking More info

On some Windows 11 computers Smart App Control blocks unsigned apps outright, with no Run anyway. It can only be turned off for every app at once (in Windows Security > App & browser control > Smart App Control settings), which you may prefer not to do; a signed installer, planned for a later version, avoids this.

Updating to a new version

Quit the app, download the new installer, and run it the same way (the confirmation step comes again for each new download). It installs the new version and removes the old one. Your history and settings are kept; if the new version brings a new map, your pages are placed on it during the next sync.

Reinstalling the version you already have is refused: uninstall it first, then install again.

Uninstalling

Uninstalling removes the program and keeps your data, so installing again later picks up where you left off.

  • Mac: quit the app, open Terminal (in Applications > Utilities), and run
    bash ~/Library/WikipediaWrapped-App/uninstall.sh
    It removes the program (~/Library/WikipediaWrapped-App) and the app in your Applications folder. Dragging the app to the Trash is not enough: it leaves the program folder, about 2 GB, behind.
  • Windows: open Settings > Apps > Installed apps (on Windows 10, Apps & features), find Wikipedia Wrapped, and choose Uninstall.

To remove your data as well, delete the data folder (above) afterwards, after copying data/visits.jsonl somewhere safe if you might want it. On a Mac, in Finder choose Go > Go to Folder and paste the folder's path; on Windows, paste %APPDATA%\WikipediaWrapped into File Explorer's address bar.

If you used the Advanced option, also remove the app's access to your Google account in your Google Account's third-party connections (myaccount.google.com/connections).
